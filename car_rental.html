Why Avis and not the cheapest

Your trip has two awkward times. You land at 07:20 on Friday and want to be driving by 08:00. On the last morning you need the car back by 07:30 on Saturday for the 09:20 flight. Most desks at CAG open at 08:00.

Recommended

Avis (also Budget and Maggiore)

  • The desk opens at 07:30. The Avis booking form offers return times from 07:30 to 23:45.
  • 24-hour key box, so you can drop early if needed (from your V2 checks).
  • Desk in the Palazzina Autonoleggi and return in the multi-storey car park next to the terminal. No shuttle bus.
  • A major brand: fewer surprise fees, clear rules, and its contract works with the Chase claim process.
Cheaper, but risky for you

B-Rent, Dryyve, Viaggiare, Felirent, Wheego…

  • Kayak's totals for 8 days start at $40–49, about $100–150 less than Avis.
  • Most are off-airport, “shuttle” or “call for pick-up”. You don't want a shuttle on a 07:30 return before a 09:20 flight.
  • When I searched with a 07:00 return, Avis and Budget disappeared from Kayak completely, so opening hours really do filter the list.
  • Budget companies often push heavy cover at the desk, take a large deposit, and some in Italy want an embossed credit card. The Sapphire is a flat metal card.
Fine for Zain, not for you

Locauto, Sicily by Car, Hertz, Europcar, Sixt

  • Desks open at 08:00. Hertz, Europcar, Goldcar, Sicily by Car and Locauto have no key box, so a 07:30 return needs their OK in writing.
  • Sixt advertises 24-hour returns (keys go to its check-in cabin) but costs more: from $166.
  • They're no cheaper than Avis for your dates anyway (see the table).

Which car

You have hand luggage only and two people, so any class fits. What matters is the roads: hairpins up to the Golgo plateau and through Baunei's narrow streets, the Genna Silana pass in the dark, 5 km of washboard dirt road into Piscinas, 1–1.5 km of dirt track to the Goloritzé trailhead, and long fast stretches on the SS131 and the new SS125.

Recommended

Renault Captur class (Compact Manual)

≈ $195 at desk · $174.82 pay now

Sits higher off the ground than a Panda, so it's calmer on the Piscinas washboard and the Golgo track. More power for the climbs, quieter on the 2–3 hour transfer days, and it holds 2 large and 2 small bags. Costs about $40 more than the Panda.

Budget pick

Fiat Panda class (Economy Manual)

≈ $156 at desk · $142.46 pay now

Sardinia's default car: narrow, nimble in village streets, cheap to run, and it handles the dirt roads fine if you go slowly. A bit noisy and slow on the SS131. A good choice if you'd rather save the $40.

Insurance with the Chase Sapphire Preferred

This is where most of the money is saved or lost. Avis Italy charges €32–58 per day for Super Cover, the counter product that takes your excess to zero. That's €256–464 for 8 days, two to three times the price of the car. Your card already covers the same risk.

What's in the Avis price by Italian law

  • Third-party liability: damage to other people, their cars and property. Every Italian rental includes it.
  • Collision damage waiver (CDW) and theft protection (TP), both marked “Included” at checkout. Each comes with an excess (the most you pay if the car is damaged or stolen), which Avis says “can be reduced by purchasing super cover at the counter”. The exact amount is written on your rental agreement. In Italy it's usually somewhere around €1,000–2,000 for a small car, so read it at the desk.

What the Chase card adds

  • Primary cover, worldwide, up to $60,000, for rentals of up to 31 days.
  • Covers theft, damage, loss-of-use charges, admin fees and towing.
  • Italy's basic CDW can't be declined, so Chase acts as secondary to it. In practice it pays back whatever Avis charges you on top: the excess.
  • You, plus any driver named on the contract, are covered.
“If the Rental Agency, outside of the United States, will not allow You to decline their CDW/LDW coverage, then this benefit is secondary to the Rental Agency’s CDW/LDW coverage.” Chase Sapphire Preferred Guide to Benefits, Auto Rental Coverage
The rules that keep you covered
  1. The whole rental goes on the Sapphire Preferred, in the main driver's name. Don't split the payment with another card or pay the balance with a debit card.
  2. Decline Super Cover (also called SCDW, “Super Copertura” or excess reduction). Buying it cancels the Chase benefit.
  3. Only named drivers. If Veronica drives without being on the contract, neither Avis nor Chase covers her.
  4. No off-road driving. Chase excludes “damage due to off-road operation” and anything that breaks the rental contract. The Piscinas road and the Golgo track are public dirt roads, not off-road, but drive them slowly. If the desk tells you unpaved roads are forbidden, ask them to explain.
  5. Not covered by Chase: injuries, your belongings, wear and tear or mechanical breakdown, and liability to other people (Italy's included insurance handles that).

The deposit (card hold)

Avis's checkout says it holds up to $300 on your card. Italian desks often block more when you decline Super Cover, sometimes the whole excess. Make sure the Sapphire has €2,000 or so of free credit. The hold is released after the car is back, and that can take up to two weeks.

If something happens

  1. Photograph everything. Get a police report or the European accident form (constatazione amichevole) if another car is involved, and call Avis.
  2. Report it to Chase within 100 days at chasecardbenefits.com, or call +1 800 350 1362 (collect from abroad: +1 214 503 2951).
  3. Send the documents within 365 days: both rental agreements, the damage report, the repair bill, photos, Avis's bill to you, and the card statement.

Avis charges the excess to your card first, and Chase pays you back weeks later.

The other add-ons at checkout

  • Windscreen Protection $3.21–3.67/day: skip it. Glass damage is damage, and Chase covers that.
  • Cover Roadside Issues $6.46/day (≈ $52): covers flat tyres, lost keys and wrong fuel. Optional. It's the one add-on I'd consider, because of the dirt roads, but I'd still skip it.
  • Additional Driver $11.23/day (≈ $90): only if Veronica drives.
  • Sat nav $15.33/day: no. Use phone offline maps.
  • Cross Border: no, you stay on the island.

Optional: a letter of coverage

Some Italian desks ask for proof that your card covers the car before they let you decline their cover. Avis usually doesn't, but it costs nothing: download a letter of coverage from chasecardbenefits.com (or ask at +1 800 350 1362) and keep the PDF on your phone.

If you'd rather never deal with a claim, the alternative is Avis Super Cover at the desk (€32–58/day). That's peace of mind at roughly €300–450, and the Chase benefit no longer applies.

Driving in Sardinia

No tolls

Sardinia has no toll motorways. The SS131, SS125 and SS554 are all free.

ZTL in Cagliari

The old centre (Castello, Marina, Stampace, Villanova) has camera-enforced restricted-traffic zones (ZTL). Park outside them and walk, especially on Thu 5 Nov for the Sella del Diavolo (Marina Piccola is fine).

Speed cameras

“Autovelox” cameras are common on the SS131, the SS125 and through villages. Usual limits: 50 in towns, 90 on ordinary roads, 110 on dual carriageways, but check the signs.

Headlights

Dipped headlights are compulsory outside towns, day and night.

Fuel

“Fai da te” (self-service) is cheaper than “servito”. Stations are rare in the mountains: Baunei is the last one before Dorgali, and there's none on the SP71 Costa del Sud (fill up before Teulada).

Parking

Blue lines: pay (machine or app). White lines: free. Yellow lines: residents or disabled only. Sinis beach parking is still paid on Mon 2 Nov.

Dirt roads

Into Piscinas via Ingurtosu (5 km of washboard) and to Su Porteddu (about 1.5 km): 20–30 km/h, keep off the deep ruts, and expect stone chips.

Animals and the dark

Goats, cattle, pigs and boar on the SS125 and the Golgo plateau, especially after sunset (about 17:20). Slow down on blind bends.
